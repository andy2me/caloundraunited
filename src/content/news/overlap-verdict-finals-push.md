*Gary, Jamie, Roy and Wrighty assemble around the table. Three flat whites, one builders tea, and a worrying amount of confidence in a team sitting 4th with the goal difference of a tossed coin.*

---

**Gary Neville:** Right, lads, let's get into it. Caloundra United. Over-35s. Currently 4th on twelve points with four games to play. Goal difference of plus nothing. Nothing. They've scored 31, conceded 31. It's the footballing equivalent of breaking even at the pokies and calling yourself a professional gambler.

**Jamie Carragher:** Gary, can I just say — fourth place. FOURTH. With a minus six losses to four wins record. This is a team that loses more than it wins and is somehow in the finals spots. That tells you everything about this league, doesn't it? It's a free-for-all from fourth down.

**Roy Keane:** *[stares into the middle distance]* I've watched the footage. I've seen Hopey. The man pulls up clutching something different every week. Hamstring. Calf. Groin. Last week I think he tweaked an eyebrow. He's now what we in the game call "a vocal presence." Which is football for "can't run, won't shut up."

**Ian Wright:** *[laughing]* Roy! Come on, man! The man's a leader! He's organising from the sideline now! That's tactical evolution, that is!

**Roy Keane:** He's organising himself into a deck chair, Ian. Permanently.

---

## A Word on Where They've Come From

**Gary Neville:** Before we get into the squad, we have to talk about the journey. Because this is a club that spent years in Division Two. YEARS. And they won that division back-to-back to get up here.

**Jamie Carragher:** That's not nothing, Gary. That is NOT nothing. Back-to-back titles in Div Two. That's a group that knows how to win things. The muscle memory of climbing out of a division — you don't lose that. They've stood on the podium. They've had the post-match beers that taste like silverware.

**Ian Wright:** And then they came up to Division One and everyone said "they'll get found out." Everyone! And here they are, fourth, fighting for finals in their first proper season at the top level! It's a fairytale, man!

**Roy Keane:** It's a fairytale until you concede 31 in 10 games.

**Ian Wright:** Roy, do you have to —

**Roy Keane:** I do, yeah.

---

**Gary Neville:** But here's the thing that nobody's mentioned. The end-of-year trip. Moreton. Scotty's boat.

*[Carragher chokes on his tea]*

**Jamie Carragher:** Gary, I've been briefed on this and I'm not going to lie, I don't think I'm allowed to repeat what I've heard. Some of it is genuinely libellous.

**Ian Wright:** They SURVIVED Scotty's boat, Gary! They came back! All of them! That's the bit nobody's talking about!

**Roy Keane:** Hold on. Hold on. Let me make sure I understand this. This is a team that, as a group, willingly boarded a vessel — captained by one of their own — went to Moreton Island, and ALL OF THEM came back alive?

**Jamie Carragher:** All of them. Apparently. Although one or two are reportedly still finding sand in places sand has no business being.

**Roy Keane:** Then a six-pointer against Buderim is NOTHING. NOTHING. If you've stared down a Scotty navigation decision and lived, you've already done the hard bit this year.

**Ian Wright:** YES ROY! THERE IT IS! THAT'S THE ENERGY!

**Gary Neville:** That's the bit that actually shifts my prediction, you know. Because look — talent gets you so far in finals football. But character, real character, that's forged in moments like that. A team that's won back-to-back promotions AND survived Scotty's boat genuinely believes it can do anything. And in finals football, belief is the difference.

**Jamie Carragher:** It's the same as that Liverpool team in Istanbul, Gary. They'd been through too much together to lose. You can't quantify it, but it's real.

**Roy Keane:** Did you just compare a Sunshine Coast over-35s team to Istanbul.

**Jamie Carragher:** I absolutely did, Roy. And I stand by it.

---

## The Squad Report

**Gary Neville:** Right. Let's go through the players, because there are storylines all over this squad. Start me with Brando — back on the park against Eumundi after god knows how long.

**Ian Wright:** Yes! Yes! The rampaging Brando is BACK! When that man's fit, he's a problem for anyone in this league. He's got the engine, he's got the bite, he carries the ball forty yards like he's still thirty-two —

**Roy Keane:** "When he's fit." That's the whole sentence, Ian. The career epitaph. They cannot — CANNOT — go into a finals push hoping Brando holds together. If they get four games out of him fit, he's the difference. If they get two, they're gone.

**Jamie Carragher:** Although Gary, I've been told the lads have made peace with his cramp routine. He drops into a downward dog mid-game now. Full yoga, full commitment, in the middle of the pitch.

**Ian Wright:** *[laughing]* The boys don't care! They DON'T care! As long as Brando stays on the park, he can do sun salutations at the halfway line for all anyone minds! Downward dog, child's pose, warrior two — whatever it takes!

**Roy Keane:** I have spent four decades in this game and I have never, EVER seen a central midfielder solve a cramp by going into a yoga pose mid-match. That is an over-35s innovation. That's going in a textbook somewhere.

**Jamie Carragher:** The lads are fine with it though. That's the point. If Brando needs to find his inner zen between phases of play to stay on the pitch, the entire squad will form a privacy circle around him while he breathes through his diaphragm.

**Ian Wright:** It's BEAUTIFUL, Roy! It's beautiful football!

**Roy Keane:** It's a man doing pilates in a centre circle.

---

**Gary Neville:** And then there's CJ. They call him Dizzy. Why do they call him Dizzy, Roy?

**Roy Keane:** I'm not asking. I don't want to know.

**Ian Wright:** Dizzy's finding his groove though! On the edge of the box he looks proper ominous. The lad's a poacher waiting to happen. Three crucial goals from him in these four games and they're in.

**Roy Keane:** IF he stays upright. Which, given the nickname, feels like a 50/50.

---

**Gary Neville:** Paul in the middle. What a player. Overloading central areas with composure you just don't see at this level.

**Jamie Carragher:** Class and calm, Gary. Class and calm. He's the metronome. And the conversation now is whether they push him back to centre-back for these final four — because if Paul plays CB and his hamstring behaves, that defence goes from "porous" to "passable" overnight.

**Roy Keane:** "If his hamstring behaves." Do you hear yourselves? Every single player on this team has a hamstring negotiation happening in real time. The medical room must look like a triage tent.

**Ian Wright:** It's over-35s football, Roy! It IS a triage tent! That's the whole league!

---

**Gary Neville:** Tinman. Or Jez. Pings a long ball like Trent Alexander-Arnold on a good day.

**Jamie Carragher:** *In his own head* he pings a long ball like Trent Alexander-Arnold, Gary. There's a difference.

**Roy Keane:** And the lad's currently nursing what's been described to me as — and I want to read this verbatim — "some type of old man hernia caused by an unorthodox toe poke."

*[long silence]*

**Roy Keane:** I've been in football for forty years. I have never, in my life, heard a more over-35s injury than that sentence.

**Ian Wright:** *[crying laughing]* An UNORTHODOX TOE POKE! Roy, please!

**Jamie Carragher:** He needs to recover physically AND mentally, apparently. Which I respect. The shame of injuring yourself with a toe poke is its own form of trauma. He'll need a sports psychologist and an apology letter to the football itself.

---

**Gary Neville:** In goal — Stevie. The Hulk. Showed incredible movement against Eumundi, low AND in the air.

**Ian Wright:** Stevie back in goals just changes the whole vibe of that team! The back four look two metres taller when he's behind them! You can SEE them defending more confidently!

**Jamie Carragher:** That's the value of a goalkeeper at this level — it's 70% the bloke between the sticks, 30% what he projects onto the back four. Stevie does both. Everyone breathes easier.

**Roy Keane:** *[grudgingly]* He's a proper keeper. I'll give him that.

**Ian Wright:** ROY GAVE HIM A COMPLIMENT! Did everyone catch that?! Roll the tape!

---

**Gary Neville:** Luke. The Dentist.

**Roy Keane:** Don't.

**Gary Neville:** I have to, Roy. The note from the bench says, and I quote, "if he can keep his back chat minimal like a good patient, we'll be ok."

**Roy Keane:** That's a player problem. That's a player who talks too much. I've played with players like that. You give them the death stare in the tunnel and they fix themselves by kick-off.

**Jamie Carragher:** Or you put them at full-back where the noise is at least directional. Down the line, into the channel — at least the back chat has tactical purpose.

**Ian Wright:** Imagine being a dentist AND a centre-half. That man has TWO careers built entirely on telling people to open up. Beautiful.

---

**Gary Neville:** Now. Baz. The Englishman. Status: file not found.

**Jamie Carragher:** Gone. Vanished. Will we ever see him in Pink and Black again? My sources say no.

**Roy Keane:** Last sighting?

**Jamie Carragher:** Reportedly on a couch. Carlsberg in hand. Watching Harry Kane. Living his best life.

**Ian Wright:** Honestly? Fair play to him. That's a man who's made peace with where he is in life.

**Roy Keane:** That's a man who's quit, Ian. There's a difference.

**Gary Neville:** Either way, you can't plan for him. He's not on the team sheet. He's a folk legend now. Pink and Black, lost to the pub.

---

**Gary Neville:** Aaron. Back from the World Cup. Reportedly arriving at the airport yelling "VAMOS COLOMBIA" at anyone who'll listen.

**Ian Wright:** *[immediately delighted]* VAMOS COLOMBIA! YES AARON! Bring that energy to Palmview, son! Bring it ALL!

**Jamie Carragher:** Gary, I love this. I love it. But here's the thing — the vibes are immaculate, the Colombian patriotism is locked in, but where is the golden boot? Because Caloundra don't need a cheerleader, they need a finisher. Pronto.

**Roy Keane:** That's exactly it. You can come back from a World Cup with all the swagger in the world. You can wear the flag round your shoulders. You can yell "Vamos" at the security guards at Brisbane Airport. But on Friday night at Palmview, you put it in the back of the net. That's the only language anyone cares about.

**Ian Wright:** But the BELIEF, Roy! He's bringing belief back with him! That stuff is contagious! You can't measure it!

**Jamie Carragher:** Belief doesn't beat Buderim, Ian. Belief plus three goals over four games beats Buderim. He needs to fire. Immediately. No bedding-in period. No "ah, let me get back to match sharpness." The team needs him at full Vamos from minute one of Round 11.

**Roy Keane:** And while we're on it — what's the Colombian thing about? Did something happen?

**Gary Neville:** I'm told he picked a side at the World Cup and committed. Fully.

**Roy Keane:** Good. That's commitment. I respect that. Now do the same thing in front of goal.

---

**Gary Neville:** Which brings us to Brackley. Back from the World Cup. And the note from the team — and I love this — is that he needs to fire his backroom staff on arrival.

**Roy Keane:** GOOD. About time someone in this team showed some standards.

**Jamie Carragher:** Brackley's physicality and touch in the middle is the single biggest thing missing from this team right now. They've been playing through a midfield that's all heart and no engine. He comes back and suddenly there's a base. Someone who can actually receive a pass under pressure.

**Ian Wright:** With Brackley AND Aaron back, that midfield three is a completely different animal. They can press, they can build, they can hurt teams. The whole shape works.

**Roy Keane:** But he needs to be ruthless. Come back, look at the prep, look at the fitness levels, and clean house. No favourites. No "well, he tries hard." This is finals football. You earn the shirt.

**Ian Wright:** Roy, it's over-35s on a Friday night.

**Roy Keane:** Standards, Ian. Standards.

---

## The Verdict

**Gary Neville:** So here's my honest read. They need a small miracle to take points off Stella or Currimundi. Both those sides are six points clear of them. They're better. But — and this is a big but — finals football is different. The pressure flips. The top teams have nothing to chase. Caloundra has everything to lose. And this is a group that's already proven, twice over, that they know how to win things.

**Jamie Carragher:** And let's be honest, with Aaron and Brackley back, the spine looks completely different. Stevie in goal. Paul at CB. Brackley screening. Aaron threading — and finishing, please god, finishing. Brando rampaging between downward dogs. CJ poaching. Suddenly that's a team. Add in Tinman's pings — IF the hernia behaves — and you've got something that can hurt anyone on the night.

**Roy Keane:** *[long pause]* I'll say this. They've got bottle. Seven-one against Coolum. Seven-two against Unity. When they turn up, they really turn up. The problem is, they also turn up and lose nil-four to Buddina. They're a Jekyll and Hyde team. That's character, but it's also chaos.

**Ian Wright:** Chaos with a chance, Roy! That's all we ever wanted in football!

---

**Gary Neville:** My prediction? They beat Coolum. They beat Buderim — just. They lose to Stella. They steal a draw off Currimundi on a wet Friday night when nobody can pass the ball. That's nineteen points. And Buderim, with their run-in, finishes on eighteen. Caloundra sneaks fourth. Finals.

**Jamie Carragher:** I'll go further. I think they win three of four. Twenty-one points. Comfortable fourth. Hopey shouts them home from a folding chair behind the goal. Brando rampages between yoga poses. Stevie keeps a clean sheet. Brackley dictates. Aaron finally remembers he's a striker and bangs in a Vamos-Colombia worldie against Buderim. The Dentist keeps quiet. Tinman pings ONE perfect Trent ball and retires happy.

**Roy Keane:** If — IF — Hopey can stop pulling up every time someone passes him the ball, and IF the World Cup boys come back fit, and IF Brando holds together between downward dogs, and IF Paul's hamstring co-operates, and IF Tinman recovers from his "unorthodox toe poke," and IF the Dentist can shut up for ninety minutes, and IF Aaron actually scores instead of just yelling Vamos, then yes. They've got a chance.

**Ian Wright:** That's the most positive thing you've ever said, Roy! That's basically a love letter!

**Roy Keane:** And Baz? Baz needs to put the Carlsberg down and find his boots.

**Ian Wright:** *[wheezing]* He's GONE, Roy! He's a ghost! He's watching Harry Kane!

**Roy Keane:** I'm leaving.

---

![Caloundra vs Buderim — Round 16, the six-pointer that decides the season](/assets/news/overlap-head-to-head.svg)

**Jamie Carragher:** Hang on, hang on, before you go, Roy — here's the thing. Two-time Division Two champions. Survived Scotty's boat to Moreton. Climbed up to Div One and sat themselves in fourth in their first proper season. If THAT group doesn't believe anything's possible, who does?

**Roy Keane:** *[from the door]* …fair point.

**Ian Wright:** HE SAID FAIR POINT! ROLL THE TAPE AGAIN!

---

**The Verdict:** Caloundra United's path to finals is narrow, but it exists. Beat Coolum. Beat Buderim. Steal something off the top two. Get the World Cup boys home, get Brackley to clean house, get Aaron's golden boot firing instead of just his vocal cords, keep Brando vertical (or downward-dogging — whatever it takes), give CJ the ball in dangerous areas, get Paul's hamstring through one more month, find Tinman a decent surgeon, put a muzzle on the Dentist, and accept that Baz is gone to the great Carlsberg in the sky.

This is a group that's won back-to-back Division Two titles and lived to tell the tale of a Scotty-captained voyage to Moreton Island. If you can survive that, you can survive a finals push.

*Fourth place. It's there for the taking. Probably. VAMOS.*
